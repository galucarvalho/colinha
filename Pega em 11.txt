    "text": "Wolfgang Kramer\n\n100 Blockhead cards, numbered from 1 to 100 10 Bull cards\n\nGame Idea\nEvery player starts with 10 cards in his hand. Each player has to place a card\non one of the stacks during his turn. You should ensure that the difference\nbetween the played card and the top card of the stack is not larger than 10,\notherwise it means “11 takes” and the player has to take the whole stack into\nhis hand. This player also gets one bull card, and with it the right to play\nmultiple cards on one stack. The goal of the game is to get rid of all your\nblockhead cards from your hand.\n\nGame Preperation\n\n¢ The blockhead cards are shuffled and each player is dealt 10 cards. Each\nplayer sorts the cards in his hand in ascending numbers. The remaining\nblockhead cards go to the middle of the table to form the draw deck.\n\n¢ The top card of the draw deck is revealed and put beside it. This forms the\nfirst Discard stack, which the blockhead cards will be placed on.\n\n¢ The bull cards also form a stack which is put on the other side of the\nblockhead draw deck, for when they are needed.\n\n¢ The youngest player starts.\n\nCard display at start of the game\n\nBull Cards Blockhead Discard\nDraw Deck Stack\n\nGameplay\nThe game plays clockwise. If its your turn you have to take one of the following\nactions:\n¢ Play a card from your hand and place it revealed on one of the stacks.\n¢ Take all the cards from one of the stacks into your hand.\n\nPlaying Cards\nThere are 3 rules for playing cards:\ne Rule 1 - Ascending\nThe played card has to be higher than the top card of a stack.\n\ne Rule 2 - Maximum difference is 10\nThe difference between the played card and the top card of a stack shouldn't\nbe greater than \"10\". If somebody has only cards, where the difference is\nhigher than 10, he has to take all cards from one stack.\n\n¢ Rule 3 - The row of numbers goes above 100 and continues with 1,2,3\nand so on\nIf the number 96 card is on top of one of the stacks, the numbers 97 to 100\nand 1 to 6 can be played there, since the difference is not bigger than 10.\nExample: The top card is \"98\". The played card is \"5\".\nThe difference is 7 (99, 100, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5).\n\nTaking a stack Into your hand\n\nA player takes this action if he doesn’t have a matching card for any of the\ndiscard stacks, or if he wishes to do so for tactical reasons.\n\nEach time a stack is taken, two cards from the drawing pile are revealed and\nplaced beside the draw deck as new stacks. This means that the number of\nstacks is continuously rising. See: “Example for a players turn”.\n\nSince there is only one stack at the beginning, you can only take that one.\nLater during the course of the game more and more stacks appear. The player\nthen can choose which one he wants to take.\n\nRemember: When a player is choosing one of the stacks, he is not allowed to\nsee what and how many cards are in there. Besides the top card of each stack,\nthe rest shouldn't be visible.\n\nThe cards of the taken stack should be sorted in your hand, so that they are in\nascending order. After that its the next players turn.\n\nImportant: Anyone who takes a stack with 3 or more cards, gets a\nbull card, which he places revealed in front of him. A player can have\nmore than one bull card.\n\nWhen the bull card pile is empty and a player has to take a bull card, he\ntakes one from the player with the most bull cards. If there is more than one\nplayer with the most cards, the player can choose between them."
